# Rules that surprise

Plain-English catalog of the game rules a football player would not expect. One rule per line; the physics behind each is in PHYSICS.md.

## 1. The ball

1.1. The ball is a cloud, not a point: every player near it has a share of it, and "having the ball" means having a big share.
1.2. The ball never runs out: if part of it fades, the rest grows to make up for it, so the total is always one ball.
1.3. The ball spreads all the time, and a small ball spreads faster than a big one: a tight ball blooms within a second.
1.4. Fast balls fade faster than slow ones (attenuation): long kicks tend to disappear sooner than slower passes and dribbles.
1.5. Two parts of the ball moving at different speeds make stripes; the bigger the speed difference, the finer the stripes.
1.6. Big stripes and a compact ball do not go together: a game with wide, readable stripes has a fuzzy, fast-spreading ball.
1.7. With coherent walls, a rebound leaves a dark band along the wall; with additive walls, the ball piles up along the walls instead.

## 2. Catching

2.1. Only the slow part of the ball is caught, slow compared with the player: running alongside the ball makes catching easy, running into it makes it hard.
2.2. Two fast balls meeting head-on in front of a player are not a still ball: they are hard to catch, even though they cancel out on average.
2.3. Some of the ball always passes through a catch zone uncaught.
2.4. A caught ball is not stopped dead: it keeps part of its speed (momentum absorbed, mean and spread), and the randomness scatters it.
2.5. Catching does not stop the ball from spreading: only Dribble's well keeps it together and in the zone.
2.6. Pass, Shot and Defense have no well: standing on a still ball in those stances does not hold it, it kicks it away bit by bit.

## 3. Kicking

3.1. A kick sends only the share that was caught: a ray leaves the zone and the rest of the ball stays, it is not the whole ball flying off.
3.2. Holding a kick stance on the ball drains it into a ray over time; a short tap sends a short pulse.
3.3. A ball just kicked is too fast for the kicker to catch again, so it is not kicked twice by the same zone.
3.4. A pass to several allies goes to all of them at once: the ball is split, not sent to one.
3.5. Rays fan out: a slow pass spreads over about plus or minus 22 degrees, a shot over about 12.
3.6. A small speed change does not split the ball: it nudges all of it (dribbling corrections stay one ball, a hard kick makes a separate ray).
3.7. With no momentum absorbed, a chain of kickers keeps adding speed; with all of it absorbed, every kick leaves at the kick speed whatever came in.

## 4. Goals and time

4.1. Nothing enters a goal: a goal is scored when enough of the ball's probability sits in the goal zone at once.
4.2. After a goal the ball keeps moving for a moment while the players are frozen.
4.3. Game seconds are frames: a slow server slows the whole game down instead of changing it.
4.4. The ball's clock runs faster than the players' (wave time speed): it does not make the players faster.
